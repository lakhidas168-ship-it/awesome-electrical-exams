# UPSC Engineering Services Examination (ESE / IES) Electrical Engineering (EE) — 5-Year Previous-Year-Question (PYQ) Pattern Analysis

**Exam:** Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) Engineering Services Examination (ESE), commonly known as **IES (Indian Engineering Services)**, **Electrical Engineering (EE)** discipline.
**Conducting body:** Union Public Service Commission (UPSC), Government of India.
**Level:** National level premier engineering examination for recruitment to Group 'A' / Group 'B' central engineering services (such as Central Engineering Service (Roads), Central Power Engineering Service, Indian Naval Armament Service, Military Engineer Services, etc.).
**Window covered:** The five most recent completed recruitment cycles — **ESE 2021, ESE 2022, ESE 2023, ESE 2024, and ESE 2025**.
**Compiled:** 2026-09-30.
**Basis:** Official UPSC ESE notifications, syllabus guidelines, annual reports, and standard engineering coaching analysis repositories (MADE EASY, ACE Engineering Academy, Gateforum).
**Integrity rule:** No URL is invented and no figure is guessed. Any point or statistical figure that could not be verified against official UPSC data is written as `UNSURE`.

## 0. Critical caveats (read first)

1. **Multi-Stage Selection Structure:** Unlike GATE (which is a single-stage online CBT exam primarily for M.Tech/PSU), ESE is a rigorous three-stage examination: **Stage I (Prelims - Objective)**, **Stage II (Mains - Descriptive/Conventional)**, and **Stage III (Personality Test / Interview)**.
2. **Dual-Paper Prelims Structure:** ESE Prelims consists of **Paper I (General Studies and Engineering Aptitude)** carrying 200 marks (common to all branches) and **Paper II (Electrical Engineering)** carrying 300 marks. Total Prelims = 500 marks.
3. **Descriptive Mains Structure:** ESE Mains consists of **two discipline-specific conventional papers** (Paper-I and Paper-II), each carrying 300 marks (Total = 600 marks), requiring rigorous mathematical derivations, circuit diagrams, and numerical step-by-step solutions without multiple-choice options.
4. **Syllabus Distinctiveness (Materials Science):** Unlike GATE EE, ESE EE explicitly incorporates **Materials Science / Electrical Materials** as a core subject in both Prelims and Mains, reflecting the manufacturing, metallurgical, and material-selection demands of public sector engineering projects.
5. **Marking & Negative Marking:** Prelims MCQ carries a negative marking of **1/3rd (0.33)** of the assigned marks for every incorrect response. Mains is evaluated on a step-marking rubric by UPSC examiners.
6. **Data Integrity Note:** Exact category-wise final recommendation cutoffs vary yearly based on vacancies and UPSC board discretion; where specific historical cutoff marks across years are subject to fluctuating category norms, they are marked `UNSURE`.

## 2. Structure & marks (Official ESE Exam Blueprint)

### Stage I: Preliminary Examination (Total: 500 Marks)
- **Paper I: General Studies and Engineering Aptitude**
  - **Duration:** 2 Hours (120 minutes)
  - **Questions:** 100 objective questions (MCQs)
  - **Maximum Marks:** 200 Marks
  - **Topics:** Current issues, Engineering Aptitude & Logical Reasoning, Engineering Mathematics & Numerical Analysis, Principles of Design/Safety, Standards & Quality Practices, Energy & Environment, Project Management, Material Science and Engineering, Ethics & Values.
- **Paper II: Electrical Engineering (Discipline-Specific)**
  - **Duration:** 3 Hours (180 minutes)
  - **Questions:** 150 objective questions (MCQs)
  - **Maximum Marks:** 300 Marks
  - **Topics:** Core Electrical Engineering curriculum (Electric Circuits, Electromagnetic Fields, Signals & Systems, Electrical Machines, Power Systems, Control Systems, Measurements, Analog & Digital Electronics, Power Electronics, Materials Science).

### Stage II: Main Examination (Total: 600 Marks)
- **Paper I: Electrical Engineering Paper-I (300 Marks, 3 Hours)**
  - Typically covers: Engineering Mathematics, Electrical Materials, Electric Circuits and Fields, Signals and Systems, Electrical Machines, Power Electronics.
- **Paper II: Electrical Engineering Paper-II (300 Marks, 3 Hours)**
  - Typically covers: Control Systems, Electrical and Electronic Measurements, Analog and Digital Electronics, Power Systems, Computer Fundamentals, Basic Electronics.
- **Format:** 8 subjective questions per paper divided into Section A and Section B. Question 1 and Question 5 are compulsory; candidates must attempt any 3 from the remaining questions (at least one from each section).

### Stage III: Personality Test (200 Marks)
- Evaluates candidate's leadership, intellectual capacity, integrity, social traits, and suitability for public engineering administration.

## 4. Recurring topics by section (PYQ-fed high-frequency map)

### 1. Electric Circuits & Field Theory
- **Network Theorems:** Thevenin, Norton, Superposition, Reciprocity applied to AC/DC circuits with dependent sources.
- **Transient Analysis:** First and second-order R-L, R-C, R-L-C circuits under step and sinusoidal excitations using Laplace transforms.
- **Two-Port Networks:** Z, Y, h, ABCD parameters, interconnection of two-port networks, image impedance.
- **Electromagnetic Fields:** Maxwell’s equations in differential and integral forms, wave propagation in dielectric and conducting media, Poynting vector, boundary conditions.

### 2. Electrical Machines
- **Transformers:** Equivalent circuit, phasor diagrams, phasor analysis, phasor groups, parallel operation, harmonics, testing (open-circuit, short-circuit, Sumpner's test).
- **DC Machines:** Armature reaction, commutation, speed control methods (Ward-Leonard, chopper-fed), braking, losses and efficiency.
- **Induction Motors:** Torque-slip characteristics, equivalent circuit parameters determination, circle diagram, starting methods, deep-bar and double-cage rotors.
- **Synchronous Machines:** Cylindrical and salient-pole machines, EMF equations, armature reaction, phasor diagrams, power-angle characteristics, V and inverted-V curves, synchronization, hunting.

### 3. Power Systems
- **Generation & Transmission:** Transmission line parameters (inductance, capacitance calculations), bundled conductors, ABCD parameters of short, medium, and long lines, surge impedance loading (SIL).
- **Fault Analysis:** Symmetrical components (positive, negative, zero sequence networks), symmetrical and unsymmetrical faults (L-G, L-L, L-L-G).
- **Load Flow & Stability:** Gauss-Seidel and Newton-Raphson load flow methods, swing equation, equal area criterion for transient stability, rotor angle stability.
- **Protection & Switchgear:** Overcurrent, distance, and differential protection relays, circuit breakers (arc interruption, rating, TRV, RRRV).
- **Economic Operation:** Economic dispatch, loss formula coefficients, load dispatch.

### 4. Control Systems
- **Mathematical Modeling:** Transfer functions, block diagram reduction, signal flow graphs (Mason’s gain formula).
- **Time Response Analysis:** Transient response specifications, steady-state error, PID controllers, Routh-Hurwitz stability criterion.
- **Frequency Response Analysis:** Bode plots, polar plots, Nyquist stability criterion, gain margin, phase margin.
- **State-Space Analysis:** State transition matrix, controllability, and observability.

### 5. Electrical & Electronic Measurements
- **Bridge Measurements:** Wheatstone, Maxwell, Hay, Anderson, Schering, Wien bridges for R, L, C measurement.
- **Instruments:** PMMC, moving iron, electrodynamometer type instruments, extension of range (shunts, multipliers), instrument transformers (CT and PT).
- **Digital Measurements & Transducers:** DVM, frequency counters, thermistors, thermocouples, LVDT, strain gauges, piezoelectric transducers.

### 6. Power Electronics & Drives
- **Power Semiconductor Devices:** Diode, SCR, TRIAC, MOSFET, IGBT — static and dynamic characteristics, triggering circuits, commutation techniques.
- **Phase-Controlled Converters:** Single-phase and three-phase rectifiers (controlled and uncontrolled) with R, R-L, and R-L-E loads.
- **Choppers & Inverters:** Step-down and step-up choppers, single-phase and three-phase voltage source inverters, PWM techniques.
- **AC & DC Drives:** Speed control of DC and AC motors using converters and inverters.

### 7. Analog & Digital Electronics & Microprocessors
- **Analog Circuits:** Operational amplifiers (inverting, non-inverting, instrumentation amplifiers, active filters, oscillators), transistor biasing and small-signal analysis.
- **Digital Systems:** Boolean algebra, logic gates, combinational circuits (multiplexers, decoders), sequential circuits (flip-flops, counters, shift registers), ADC and DAC converters.
- **Microprocessors:** Architecture of 8085 (pin configuration, instruction set, addressing modes, interrupts) and basic 8086 concepts.

### 8. Materials Science
- **Dielectric Properties:** Polarization mechanisms, dielectric loss, dielectric breakdown.
- **Magnetic Properties:** Diamagnetism, paramagnetism, ferromagnetism, ferrimagnetism, soft and hard magnetic materials, hysteresis losses.
- **Semiconductor Materials:** Energy bands, carrier concentration, intrinsic/extrinsic semiconductors, Hall effect.
- **Conducting & Superconducting Materials:** Material resistivity, factors affecting resistivity, Type-I and Type-II superconductors, BCS theory basics.

## 5. Question types, marking scheme, and test structure

- **Prelims (Objective):**
  - Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs) with 4 options (A, B, C, D).
  - Marking: +1 or +2 marks per correct question depending on section/weight, with a negative marking of **1/3rd (0.33)** of the assigned mark for incorrect attempts.
- **Mains (Descriptive / Conventional):**
  - Pen-and-paper written format.
  - Multi-part subjective questions (sub-parts a, b, c) testing derivations, numerical problem-solving, and theoretical explanations.
  - Step-marking is enforced: logical progression, correct sign conventions, proper unit usage, and clear schematic diagrams receive proportional credit even if the final numerical calculation contains minor arithmetic errors.

## 7. Recommended PYQ strategy & preparation synthesis

1. **Master the Core Pillars:** Power Systems and Electrical Machines constitute over 30% of the combined Prelims and Mains weightage. Mastery here is non-negotiable.
2. **Rigorous Conventional Practice (Mains):** ESE Mains rewards structured derivations and clean circuit diagrams. Practicing past 10 years' descriptive questions without looking at solutions builds the stamina required for the 3-hour descriptive paper.
3. **Materials Science & GS Paper I:** Do not neglect Paper I (General Studies) and Materials Science. While engineers tend to focus solely on core electrical subjects, Paper I often decides prelims qualification.
4. **Numerical Speed & Accuracy:** Prelims Paper II demands 150 questions in 180 minutes (~72 seconds per question). Quick formula recall and approximation techniques are vital.
